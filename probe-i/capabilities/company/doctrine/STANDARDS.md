# Standards doctrine — what may be claimed, and what may be copied

Read this before naming any standard in a client deliverable, and before putting a standard's text
anywhere — a report, a scaffold, a slide, a prompt, or this plugin's own code.

Two independent questions, and confusing them is the trap:

1. **May the practice claim conformity?** Usually yes, in a specific form. Certification, almost
   never.
2. **May the practice reproduce the text?** Usually **no**, and the terms tightened in May 2026 in
   a way that names consultancy deliverables explicitly.

Everything here was verified against the publishers' own pages on 2 September 2026. Anything not
verified is marked **unverified** and must not be presented to a client as established.

---

## 1. There is no certification for brand, reputation or communications work

Not "hard to get". None exists.

| Standard | Requirements or guidance | Certification scheme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 10668:2010 — brand valuation | **Requirements** | **None** |
| ISO 20671-1:2021 — brand evaluation, principles | Principles / meta-standard | **None** |
| ISO 20671-2:2023 — implementation and reporting | **Requirements** | **None** |
| ISO 37000:2021 — governance of organizations | **Guidance** | **None** |
| ISO 26000:2010 — social responsibility | **Guidance, no requirements** | **None, and ISO says a claim would be a misuse** |
| ISO 31000:2018 — risk management | **Guidelines** | **None** |
| ISO 10001–10004:2018 — customer satisfaction | **Guidelines** | **None** |
| ISO 9001:2015 — quality management | **Requirements** | **Yes** |
| ISO 37001:2025 — anti-bribery | **Requirements** | **Yes** |
| ISO 37301:2021 — compliance management | **Requirements** | **Yes** |

Only three are certifiable, all are management-system standards, and **none of them is about brand
or communications.**

Stated as flatly as it can be: **there is no accreditation body for communications, brand or social
audit methodologies anywhere in this landscape.** The personal credentials that exist — the US
APR, administered by a self-constituted board whose own external accreditation could not be
verified, and CIPR Chartership, which does rest on a Royal Charter — **accredit a person, never a
work product.** No page of either body describes accreditation of a methodology, an audit, a report
or a firm.

**GRI says it of itself, verbatim:** *"GRI is not an auditor, assessor, certifier, or verifier of
sustainability reports… GRI does not certify or verify an organization's products, services, or
reports."* It certifies **people** (a "GRI Certified Sustainability Professional", valid one year)
and **training partners** — never a report.

So the only defensible framing is: **"audit conducted against [named, dated, cited framework] by
[named credentialed practitioner]."** Never "accredited audit", never "certified audit", never
"verified".

The structural proof, rather than an argument: a real certification scheme has a companion standard
governing the bodies that audit against it. ISO/TC 309 has one in development for ISO 37301.
**ISO/TC 289 — the brand-evaluation committee — has none, across its entire nine-item portfolio.**

Two further facts that close the door:

- **No ISO standard on reputation management or corporate communication exists at all.** ISO's own
  site search for "corporate communication" returns telecom standards about corporate
  *telecommunication networks*.
- **There is no accreditation for organic social engagement measurement.** The Media Rating
  Council's accreditations attach to paid-advertising measurement — ad impressions, viewability,
  invalid traffic. None covers organic engagement or any engagement rate.

### The two sentences worth memorising

Both are the publishers' own words, both free to read, and both do more work in a client
conversation than any argument the practice could make itself.

> **ISO 26000's own abstract:** *"ISO 26000:2010 is not a management system standard. It is not
> intended or appropriate for certification purposes or regulatory or contractual use. Any offer to
> certify, or claims to be certified, to ISO 26000 would be a misrepresentation of the intent and
> purpose and a misuse of ISO 26000:2010."*

Note it rules out **contractual** use too — relevant if a client wants the standard named in a
statement of work.

> **ISO's own FAQ for ISO 31000:** *"ISO 31000 cannot be used for certification purposes, but does
> provide guidance for internal or external **audit programmes**."*

That second one is the practice's licence to operate. ISO itself endorses using a standard to frame
an audit programme while forbidding the certification claim. That is exactly the posture required.

### ISO does not certify anyone

ISO, verbatim: *"ISO does not perform certification or issue certificates, and it does not permit
anyone to use the ISO logo in connection with certification. Certification is performed by external
certification bodies, thus a company or organization cannot be certified by ISO."*

- **Certification** is an independent body's written assurance that something meets specific
  requirements.
- **Accreditation** is formal recognition that a certification body operates to international
  standards.
- **The ISO logo is a registered trademark and may never appear on a client deliverable.**

**Update most methodologies have not caught up with: IAF and ILAC no longer exist as such.**
Global Accreditation Cooperation Incorporated (Global ACI) has assumed the former roles of the
International Accreditation Forum and the International Laboratory Accreditation Cooperation. Any
audit methodology naming IAF as the verification authority is out of date. ISO's page still
references both Global ACI's succession and IAF CertSearch, so the transition is mid-flight —
**verify the current branding before publishing methodology text.**

## 2. The reproduction trap — this one has teeth

ISO's licence terms were **updated 2026-05-29**. Content is licensed, not sold: a
*"non-exclusive, non-transferable, limited and revocable licence"* tied to the **specific edition**
purchased, with electronic copies watermarked with the licensee's account information including
their email address.

The base licence grants the right to *"access, read, consult, and internally reference"* and to
*"understand, evaluate, and apply its content within the Licensee's **internal** operations"* —
note how many times "internal" appears — and explicitly **excludes** *"rights of reproduction,
distribution, adaptation, incorporation, digital integration, or creation of derivative works."*

**Section 6(a) requires an additional licence to reproduce or incorporate ISO content, in whole or
in part, into ISO's own enumerated list:**

- *"internal or external reports"*
- *"manuals, procedures, training materials, or guidance documents"*
- *"presentations or communication materials"*
- *"publications, products, or services made available to third parties"*

**That list names a consultancy's entire output.** Pasting clause text, a lettered sub-list, a
derived checklist table or a table of contents into a client report or a deck requires a separate
licence from ISO, a member body, or an authorised distributor.

**Section 6(b)** extends the same requirement to digital integration — embedding standards content
in software, rules engines, compliance systems, databases, structured datasets, **automated or
AI-enabled tools**, or digital platforms.

**Section 7(b) prohibits AI and machine-learning use without a separate licence** — training,
fine-tuning, embedding, prompting, querying, summarising, reformulating, text-and-data-mining, or
benchmarking against ISO content. **ISO expressly opts out of the EU text-and-data-mining
exception** (Article 4, Directive (EU) 2019/790).

### What this means for this plugin, concretely

- **A scaffold, a doctrine file, or a skill may cite a designation, a title and a clause number.**
  Those are facts, not protected expression.
- **None of them may contain a clause's text**, a lettered requirement list, or a checklist derived
  by restating clauses.
- **A purchased ISO PDF must never be given to a model** to generate audit criteria, summarise
  requirements, or build a scoring rubric. That is the prohibited use, named explicitly.
- **A single-user licence covers one named person.** ISO: the publication *"may not be shared with
  any other person or stored on shared systems or networks **even within the Licensee's legal
  entity**."* **A purchased standard therefore must not go into a company's shared store** — which
  is otherwise where everything in this plugin lives. It is the one document class that stays out.

Enforcement is published, not theoretical: ISO reserves the right to audit the licensee at premises
or remotely, to invoice underpaid fees **plus the full cost of the audit**, and applies a **fee
uplift of three times the applicable licence fee per breach** of the non-disclosure obligations,
which **survive termination**. Governing law Switzerland, jurisdiction Geneva.

### Reading them free is essentially not possible

The free online preview publishes only informative sections — foreword, introduction, scope, terms
and definitions — and sometimes the table of contents. ISO's own footer on every preview:
*"Only informative sections of standards are publicly available."* Preview depth is inconsistent:
ISO 10668, ISO 20671-1 and ISO 9001 expose full clause-level contents; ISO 37000 and ISO 20671-2
expose none.

The former Publicly Available Standards list has been **retired**. The only free items found across
this whole set are two trivial "Climate action changes" amendments at CHF 0.

Buying every standard surveyed here, at single-user prices, comes to roughly **CHF 2,270 — for one
named user each.** Distributor pricing runs higher: one standard is CHF 196 from ISO and USD 321
from its US distributor.

**The practical consequence:** build the audit's structure from sources that permit reproduction —
the AAPOR disclosure items, the Barcelona Principles, the MRC guidelines' free PDF, published
peer-reviewed work — and cite ISO by designation only, where citing it adds something.

## 3. The wording ladder

Strongest claim to weakest. Pick the strongest row the evidence actually supports, and never a row
above it.

| Phrase | What it asserts | Safe? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Certified to ISO X" | An accredited third party issued a certificate | **Only for ISO 9001 / 37001 / 37301, and only if a real certification body did it.** Never for brand or communications |
| "Audited against ISO X" | A formal audit was performed | **Risky** — implies auditor competence and independence. Avoid unless both can be evidenced |
| **"Conducted in accordance with ISO X:YYYY"** | The work followed the standard | **Safe. ISO's own construction, and the strongest defensible claim** |
| "Conforming to the processes of ISO X:YYYY" | Same, at method level | **Safe** — verbatim ISO 20671-2 phrasing |
| "Based on / informed by ISO X:YYYY" | The standard was an input | **Safe**, weaker |
| "Aligned with ISO X:YYYY" | Loose consistency | **Safe** but vague enough to be near-meaningless — prefer "in accordance with" where it can be evidenced |

Three independent constraints apply to every row:

1. **Never imply third-party attestation.** No "certified", "accredited", "approved", "licensed",
   and never the ISO logo.
2. **Never reproduce the text.** Designation, title and clause number are citable. Clause bodies
   are not.
3. **Always date the designation.** "ISO 10668:2010", never "ISO 10668".

### Why dating is not pedantry

- **`ISO 20671:2019` is withdrawn** (stage 95.99), revised by `ISO 20671-1:2021`. A proposal citing
  the 2019 designation cites a dead one. The 2021 edition is a *minor revision* whose only change
  was renumbering, so the 2019 text is still correct content under the wrong name.
- **`ISO 9001:2015` is being replaced by `ISO 9001:2026`, expected September 2026** — this month, as
  this is written. ISO's own page says the 2015 edition is *"about to be withdrawn"*. **Clause
  numbers can move between editions**, so never hard-code a clause number without its dated
  designation.
- `ISO 10668:2010` and `ISO 31000:2018` both sit at stage 90.92, "to be revised".

### AA1000, where the line is different

The AccountAbility standards — AA1000AP 2018, AA1000SES 2015, and the AA1000 Assurance Standard —
are free to download after a registration form, and are **© All rights reserved with no open
licence published.** Free to download is not free to republish.

Their restriction is on the **product**, not the reading: *"only AccountAbility-licensed assurance
providers can conduct assurance engagements using the AA1000AS."*

- **Permitted:** reading, applying, and referencing. "Stakeholder engagement designed in line with
  AA1000SES 2015" or "reviewed against the AA1000 AccountAbility Principles 2018".
- **Prohibited:** performing an AA1000AS assurance engagement, issuing an assurance statement,
  calling the work "AA1000 assurance", or implying licensed status.

And note what an AA1000 licence is: **AccountAbility licenses providers itself. There is no
accreditation body behind it.** It is a publisher licence, and must never be presented as
equivalent to accredited certification. The commonly cited designation "AA1000AS v3 (2020)" is
**unverified** — the publisher's own pages give no version or year.

## 4. The house sentence

Use this, or something that carries the same three parts, wherever a standard is named in a client
deliverable:

> This audit was conducted in accordance with **ISO 10668:2010** and **ISO 20671-1:2021**. Neither
> standard is subject to any ISO or accredited third-party certification scheme; ISO does not
> perform certification. No certification, accreditation or third-party attestation is claimed or
> implied.

Three parts, and each is load-bearing: the **dated designation**, the statement that **no scheme
exists**, and the **explicit disclaimer of attestation**. It is defensible, it cites a primary
source, and it pre-empts the challenge rather than waiting for it.

## 6. Known gaps

- **ISO 37000:2021's named governance principles** could not be verified — its free preview
  publishes no clause list. The three-tier architecture (one primary, then foundational, then
  enabling) and the three outcomes (effective performance, responsible stewardship, ethical
  behaviour) **were** verified from the free introduction.
- **ISO 20671-1:2021's five brand-strength dimension names** are paywalled. That there are exactly
  five is verified from a sibling standard's free introduction. The five **elements** are verified:
  tangible resources, quality, service, innovation, intangible resources. Note that a widely
  repeated version of this list substitutes "investment" and "sustainability" for two of them and is
  **wrong** — investment is what an entity puts into the elements, and sustainability is the subject
  of a separate, still-unpublished part.
- **ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.4's verbatim lettered requirements** are paywalled. The clause number
  and its title, "Communication", are verified.
- **Brand-equity models remain partly unverified.** Keller's customer-based brand equity is soundly
  cited (*Journal of Marketing* 57, January 1993, 1–22) but its component structure was not
  confirmed against the primary text; Aaker's *Managing Brand Equity* (1991) and the Brand Equity
  Ten are cited, but the five-asset-category taxonomy was not verified and must not be conflated
  with the ten-attribute measurement set. **Reproducing either model's published figure is a
  copyright act** — cite and describe, or draw an original structure and attribute the model.
- **The communication-audit lineage is now resolved, and the suspicion was correct.** The audit
  methodology does **not** come from a professional communicators' association — it comes from the
  academic literature. The citable backbone is Hargie & Tourish (eds), *Auditing Organizational
  Communication*, Routledge, 2nd ed. 2009, whose methodology chapters name distinct methods with
  their authors: questionnaire, interview, focus group, log-sheet, social network analysis, and
  crafting the audit report. **Do not attribute an audit methodology to a professional association
  without checking that it publishes one — several widely-repeated attributions are wrong**,
  including one crediting a commercial academic book to a professional institute, and one crediting
  the Global Capability Framework to the wrong researcher.
- **ISO 26000's clause numbers for stakeholder engagement and communication could not be
  verified** — the free preview truncates before them. Its *scope* is verified and directly on
  point: it covers *"identifying and engaging with stakeholders"* and *"communicating commitments,
  performance and other information."* **Buy the standard before citing a clause number.**
- **AA1000 assurance-provider fees and competence criteria** sit behind the licensing platform.
