# Profile — the operating archetype, and why the method bends to it

Read before phase 1 of the operación track. It carries the four questions that place any company,
the six archetypes they resolve to, what each one changes downstream, and the rule for a trade
nobody in the room understands.

`01-empresa.md` governs asking for documents. `SESSION.md` governs asking a person anything. This file
governs only one thing: **deciding what kind of business this is before spending a session mapping
it as if it were another kind.**

## 1 · Why a sector label is not a profile

`BRAND.md` already carries a `Sector` field and `REGULATORS-MX.md` maps it to a regulator. That is
the label's whole job and it does it well. It answers *which authority can fine them*. It does not
answer *what this company's day looks like*, and those are different questions with different
consequences.

Two companies filed under the same sector run on different clocks. A veterinary practice and a body
shop share nothing in their SIC code and everything in their operation: an appointment book that
**is** the capacity, no-shows that eat the margin, a customer who does not come back because nobody
called. The inventory a phase-3 session should hunt for is nearly identical. Meanwhile two
companies both filed as "construcción" — one building houses, one selling and fitting finishes over
the counter — need entirely different sessions.

So the profile is not the sector. It is the **operating archetype**, and it is derived, not asked.

## 2 · The four questions

Ask these before choosing anything. The answers are worth more than the label they produce.

| # | Question | Why it decides something |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **How does the money come in?** One-off project · subscription or retainer · repeated transaction · billed hours · margin on goods · commission | Sets what a "unit" is, and therefore what every later count counts |
| 2 | **How long does one sale take?** Minutes · days · weeks · months | Decides whether follow-up is a process or a reflex, and whether a pipeline exists at all |
| 3 | **What is delivered?** A physical good · a service performed on site · a service performed remotely · software · intermediation | Decides where the evidence lives and whether the doers are reachable at a desk |
| 4 | **What stops them selling twice as much tomorrow?** Demand · delivery capacity · administration · working capital | **The one that decides what is worth automating at all** |

Question 4 is the one the owner answers worst, and it is the one that matters most. Write down what
they say, mark it as their claim, and **contradict it against evidence in phase 2 rather than in
conversation.** An owner who says "we need more clients" while sitting on forty unquoted enquiries
has a delivery problem wearing a demand costume, and no amount of lead generation fixes it.

## 3 · The six archetypes

| | Archetype | Money | Cycle | The constraint that is usually real |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | **Bespoke project** | High ticket, few operations, staged payments | Weeks to months | Quoting capacity and follow-up discipline |
| **B** | **Recurring service** | Monthly recurring | Medium sale, long tenure | Cost to serve, and churn nobody sees coming |
| **C** | **High-volume transaction** | Low ticket, high count, margin on goods | Minutes | Stock, shrinkage, and the daily close |
| **D** | **Appointment service** | Per session; the book **is** the capacity | Hours to days | No-shows and unfilled cancellations |
| **E** | **Make-to-order production** | Per order, with identifiable materials and labour | Days to weeks | Costing accuracy and floor load visibility |
| **F** | **Distribution and logistics** | Margin on volume | Recurring against a customer book | Working capital: days of stock plus days of receivables |

### What each archetype changes downstream

This is the whole point of the profile. Everything below is what phase 3 goes hunting for.

| Archetype | The support activities most often forgotten | Where the durable evidence lives | The first quantity worth having |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Change control, staged collections, closing a job | Quotes, contracts, change orders | Quotes issued per month, and how many became work |
| **B** | Onboarding a new client, the periodic deliverable, renewal | Recurring invoices, support tickets | Clients served, and hours per client per month |
| **C** | Reordering, the daily close, shrinkage and returns | Point-of-sale records, purchase orders | Transactions per day and stock turns |
| **D** | Filling a cancellation, chasing the customer who stopped coming | The appointment book | Book occupancy and no-show rate |
| **E** | Costing after the fact, material purchasing, floor scheduling | Production orders, material receipts | Orders per month and real versus quoted cost |
| **F** | Credit authorisation, route building, order re-keying | Delivery notes, invoices, statements | Days of stock and days of receivables |

`03-procesos/`'s support-activity checklist is deliberately generic. This table is what makes it
specific, and specificity is the difference between an inventory that names twelve processes and one
that names nineteen.

## 4 · Mixtures, and which one wins

Most real companies are two archetypes. A builder that also sells finishes is **A + C**. A clinic
with a product counter is **D + C**. A bookkeeping practice with special projects is **B + A**.

The rule: **document both, then map the one with the higher margin first.** Not the higher revenue —
the higher margin. A retail line can carry more turnover and less contribution than the service line
beside it, and mapping the turnover first produces a shortlist that optimises the wrong half of the
business.

Say the mixture aloud and get it confirmed: *"so this behaves more like X than like Y — right?"*
That single sentence corrects more wrong assumptions than any question in this file.

## 5 · A trade nobody in the room understands

It happens, and pretending otherwise wastes the session. The order is fixed:

1. **Answer the four questions.** They are trade-independent and usually enough on their own.
2. **Read about the trade before the session, not during it.** What its value chain looks like, what
   its unit of measure is, what software the trade actually runs in this country, what regulator
   touches it, what its standard metrics are. Half an hour of this changes what an hour of interview
   is worth, and it stops the consultant asking the question that reveals they have never seen the
   business before.
3. **Ask for the two sequences that exist in every company**, which need no trade knowledge at all:
   *"walk me through one peso, from the moment a customer decides to buy until it is in your
   account"* and *"walk me through one order, from the moment it arrives until the customer is
   satisfied."* Narrated end to end, those two produce the skeleton of any operation.
4. **Write the archetype down as a new one** if none of the six fits, using the same columns as §3.
   Phase 4 has nothing to prioritise against otherwise.

**What is read before the session is a hypothesis about the trade, never a fact about this company.**
Mark it as such in `00-PERFIL.md` and confirm every line of it in the room. A consultant who arrives
with a printed list of "your processes" and reads it out has replaced a diagnosis with a template.

## 6 · The glossary, and why it is not courtesy

Every trade has words that mean something specific and something else outside it. Capturing them is
not politeness; it is the thing that stops a whole inventory being wrong.

`03-procesos/` already requires the process be named *in the doers' words*, and `01-empresa.md` records
what the company calls each person. Both rules assume the words have been captured somewhere. This
is that somewhere.

Two prompts do most of the work: *"what words do you use here that someone from outside wouldn't
understand?"* and, every time a term goes past that is not fully understood, **stop and ask**. A
term half-understood in phase 1 becomes a mis-scoped automation in phase 5, and by then it is
expensive.

Record the term, what it means here, and — when it differs — what the same word means elsewhere.
That last column is where the real errors hide.

## 7 · What the profile is not allowed to do

- **It does not replace the inventory.** Nothing in §3 is a captured process. Every line of it is a
  place to look, and a place to look that turns up nothing is a finding worth recording.
- **It does not set the price.** The quote comes from exception rows and integration surfaces, which
  are phases 3 and 4. An archetype is not a scope.
- **It does not survive contradiction.** If phase 2's evidence says the company behaves like a
  different archetype, the archetype changes and the session that assumed the old one gets its
  assumptions re-checked. Record the change with its date rather than editing the old line away.
